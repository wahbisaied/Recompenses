Project Overview: Provide a brief description of your project, outlining its main objectives, key activities, and expected impacts. (Word limit:150):
On November 1, 2026, JCI Sahline organized a comprehensive rabies vaccination campaign in front of the Sidi Amer Municipal headquarters. Responding to heightened national rabies alerts, our team mobilized three licensed veterinarians, four senior veterinary technicians, an intern, and municipal services to provide free vaccinations and clinical examinations. JCI volunteers managed community outreach, public registration, queue logistics, and owner guidance. The campaign successfully welcomed 200 animals (118 cats, 80 dogs, 2 horses) benefiting 136 families. Supported by a regional broadcast on Radio Monastir and targeted social media campaigns, the project eliminated financial barriers to preventive animal healthcare, prevented devastating zoonotic outbreaks, and reinforced community health resilience.

Program Objectives and Economic Impact

Q1: What were the primary goals of the program? (Word limit:200):
The program pursued four primary objectives. First, establishing a direct epidemiological barrier against rabies transmission in Sidi Amer through free, high-volume vaccination of domestic pets and working animals. Second, delivering community education on rabies symptoms, bite protocol, and responsible pet hygiene, breaking the cycle of misinformation in peri-urban neighborhoods. Third, building an effective operational coalition uniting municipal leadership, the regional agricultural commissariat (CRDA), veterinary specialists, and youth scouts around community health. Fourth, alleviating the economic burden on low-income households who would otherwise incur private veterinary fees or face catastrophic costs associated with post-exposure human medical treatments.

Q2: Describe how the project prioritized economic development within the community, differentiating it from charitable or humanitarian initiatives. (Word limit:200):
Unlike passive humanitarian charity, this initiative served as an economic risk-mitigation strategy protecting community productivity and household capital. Rabies outbreaks create severe economic externalities: mandatory quarantine, loss of working draft horses and guarding livestock (essential for local smallholders and farmers), and expensive post-exposure prophylaxis regimens that drain family finances. Welcoming 2 horses, 80 dogs, and 118 cats across 136 households safeguarded agricultural and household security assets. By preventing disease transmission at the source, JCI Sahline protected family livelihoods and averted localized economic shocks. Furthermore, the event catalyzed civic trust and economic footfall around the municipal center, laying the groundwork for ongoing biosecurity partnerships.

Planning, Execution, and Financial Oversight

Q3: Describe the execution process. How were the project's activities coordinated to align with the financial goals? (Word limit:200):
Execution adhered to a four-stage operational plan designed for maximum cost efficiency. Phase 1 involved institutional liaison: securing municipal authorization from the Mayor of Sidi Amer and partnering with the Sub-regional Administration of Animal Production of Sahline to provide medical vaccines and technical personnel. Phase 2 executed an intensive marketing campaign: distributing street posters, producing promotional videos, and broadcasting a feature interview on Radio Monastir. Phase 3 delivered the field clinic: JCI members established an outdoor reception grid, managed owner registration, coordinated animal flow, and assisted veterinary practitioners. Phase 4 recorded follow-up requests for home-bound owners. Sharing logistical responsibilities ensured all financial objectives were achieved without chapter debt.

Q4: What financial challenges were encountered, and how were they addressed during the implementation? (Word limit:200):
The primary financial challenge was the high market cost of commercial veterinary vaccines, professional clinical supplies, and event infrastructure, which far exceeded a local chapter's independent operating budget. JCI Sahline overcame this by building a non-cash institutional alliance: public veterinary services supplied the vaccines and technical supervision; the Sidi Amer Municipality provided outdoor logistical space, security, and municipal equipment at zero cost; and JCI members developed digital promotional assets in-house. This multi-sector resource mobilization enabled the complete delivery of high-value veterinary care to 136 households without imposing any fee on citizens or straining chapter funds.

Q5: Evaluate the financial management throughout the project. How effectively were the budget and financial resources managed to achieve the desired outcomes? (Word limit:200):
Financial management was exceptionally disciplined, driven by resource leverage rather than cash expenditure. Direct monetary outlays were restricted to minor organizational and signage expenses, which were absorbed within the chapter's civic engagement budget. By securing professional medical labor, institutional vaccine stockpiles, and public venue space entirely through in-kind partnerships, the campaign generated thousands of dinars in real community economic value while maintaining balanced books. Complete expenditure ledgers and partner resource confirmations were audited by the local board.

Long-term impact / Evaluation

Structurally, it created a replicable community vaccination framework that municipal authorities plan to reactivate annually in partnership with JCI Sahline.

Q15: What were the key learnings from the project, and what were the biggest challenges that had to be overcome? (Word limit:200):
