Project objectives, planning, and execution

Q1: To what extent does the program succeed in actively engaging citizens in civic initiatives?
The October Rose initiative mobilized approximately 55 active citizens, engaging both women and men in a vital civic healthcare cause. By breaking cultural taboos surrounding breast cancer in a welcoming, community-centered venue (Hoist Gym), the program successfully involved citizens not merely as passive spectators, but as proactive participants in physical wellness, preventative self-examination, and psychological discussions. Furthermore, through our viral digital campaign, the initiative engaged tens of thousands of citizens online, transforming individual health awareness into a collective civic responsibility.

Q2: How has cooperation with the government, government agencies, NGOs, or civic groups contributed to the program's effectiveness?
Cooperation with diverse civic organizations was central to the program's success. JCI Sahline partnered with the Union de la Femme (UNFT Sahline) to mobilize local women and families, and with Sport pour Tous to integrate physical wellness into cancer prevention. Crucially, we partnered with three volunteer medical specialists: Dr. Ines Ben Saad (Emergency Medicine), Dr. Adnen Hanchi (Occupational Medicine), and Dr. Farid Zaafran (Psychiatry). This multi-sector alliance ensured clinical rigor, civic trust, and holistic support addressing physical, medical, and emotional health.

Q3: What added value has this collaboration brought?
The civic coalition delivered extraordinary added value by combining medical expertise, sports facilities, community trust, and private-sector generosity. Hoist Gym provided their commercial sports facility, team, and fitness coaches (Menyar Ben Amor, Hassen Zribi, and Samir Chouari) free of charge. Event decorator Hadil Events contributed significant discounts on materials. Volunteer doctors provided free expert clinical guidance, while journalist Sami Mansour provided high-profile regional media coverage. This collaboration elevated a local awareness day into a multi-disciplinary civic festival without financial cost to attendees.

Q4: Has the program achieved its initial objectives?
Yes, the program achieved its core objectives: delivering direct, stigma-free screening awareness and preventative health counseling to 55 participants on site, offering personalized access to medical specialists, and broadcasting preventative education across regional digital channels. The initiative successfully fostered open dialogues on early detection, reinforced healthy physical lifestyles, and generated community solidarity around women's health.

Q5: To what extent does the project contain objectively verifiable indicators for evaluating results and include a clear evaluation plan?
The project evaluated success through verifiable quantitative and digital indicators: approximately 55 registered on-site participants; three specialist physicians delivering consultations; two fitness coaches leading wellness sessions; a Facebook Live broadcast by journalist Sami Mansour; and verified Meta analytics documenting 61,399 post views, 29,849 unique viewers, 1,446 interactions, and 7,400 Instagram impressions. Post-event clinical inquiries and participant feedback were monitored to evaluate community resonance.

Q6: Is the project's implementation well structured, effective, and consistent with the established plan?
Implementation adhered strictly to a well-structured plan directed by Imen Agrebaoui. The schedule encompassed four phases: establishing institutional and medical partnerships; launching targeted digital and poster promotions; executing the open-day event at Hoist Gym featuring doctor-led talks, live demonstrations, and coaching sessions; and conducting post-event digital follow-up and media dissemination. All activities proceeded smoothly within scheduled timelines and budget allocations.

Member Participation

Q7: How many JCI members actively participated in the project?
Eight JCI Sahline members actively planned, coordinated, and executed the October Rose program.

Q8: What was their role in the design, implementation, and follow-up?
Action Director Imen Agrebaoui oversaw general concept design, medical doctor recruitment, and institutional partnership coordination. Team members managed venue setup and stage decoration at Hoist Gym, handled participant registration and reception, coordinated the physician consultation schedule, assisted fitness coaches, and supervised digital live-streaming and media coverage alongside journalist Sami Mansour.

Q9: How were members motivated or mobilized throughout the program?
Members were motivated by the profound humanitarian and civic significance of combating breast cancer. Team meetings assigned operational responsibilities matching member strengths—ranging from medical logistics to public communications. The shared purpose of serving local women and families fostered high enthusiasm, accountability, and team cohesion.

Community Impact

Q10: What impact did the program have on strengthening the capacities and skills of community members? (How was this impact measured?)
The program empowered attendees with practical, lifesaving competencies in breast self-examination techniques, recognition of early clinical symptoms, and navigation of public screening facilities. Participants also gained psychological coping strategies for managing family health anxiety through Dr. Farid Zaafran's mental health workshop, measured through active Q&A engagement and direct participant feedback.

Q11: Did the project respond to a real or urgent need identified in the community?
Yes, breast cancer represents the leading oncology risk among women in Tunisia, yet awareness and routine screening remain hindered in smaller peri-urban communities by social stigma, fear, and lack of accessible specialist consultations. Providing free, direct access to doctors and coaches in an informal, welcoming gym environment directly answered an urgent public health imperative.

Q12: What image did your organization leave with the community through this program?
JCI Sahline projected the image of a dynamic, compassionate, and socially conscious organization deeply committed to community welfare. Attendees and partners praised JCI as an innovative civic leader capable of uniting private businesses, doctors, and civic associations in a dignified, empowering environment.

Q13: How did this project strengthen your brand awareness and the community's confidence in participating in your future projects?
The exceptional digital resonance—achieving 61,399 views on Facebook with 93.3% reach among non-followers and 7,400 views on Instagram—dramatically increased JCI Sahline's brand recognition. The endorsement by reputable doctors and prominent journalist Sami Mansour solidified public trust, leading to increased attendance at subsequent chapter activities.

Long-term impact of the program

Q14: Will any partnerships continue after this collaboration?
Yes, collaborative relationships with Hoist Gym, Sport pour Tous, the Union de la Femme, and participating medical practitioners have been formalized into recurring community partnerships, establishing an annual collaborative framework for ongoing health and wellness initiatives.

Q15: Has the project opened the door to other ideas or initiatives with the same partners?
Yes, partner discussions have initiated plans for follow-up preventative wellness days, including adolescent health forums, workplace ergonomics seminars with Dr. Adnen Hanchi, and community fitness challenges co-hosted with Hoist Gym and Sport pour Tous.

Q16: What measures have been taken to ensure the sustainability of the project?
JCI Sahline institutionalized October Rose as a permanent annual fixture in its civic action calendar. Operational manuals, contact rosters of volunteer medical specialists, and partnership protocols have been archived to ensure seamless execution and scaling by future chapter leadership teams.
