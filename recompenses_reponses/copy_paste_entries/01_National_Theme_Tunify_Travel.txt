Objectives, planning, finances, and execution

Q1: To what extent is the project relevant to the objectives and priorities of the target category?
Tunify Travel directly aligns with the 2026 National Theme: "Tunisia, Capital of Arab Tourism: Heritage, Business and Discovery." While Tunisia attracts millions of visitors, travel information remains highly fragmented across disconnected sources, leaving cultural heritage sites, regional artisanal attractions, and local hospitality providers under-visited. Tunify Travel addresses this challenge by providing an integrated digital solution featuring interactive geospatial mapping, curated cultural discovery, and structured travel itineraries. By connecting travelers directly with local businesses, accommodations, and transport providers, our project stimulates local economic activity while promoting Tunisian cultural heritage. Tunify Travel proves that young active citizens can build innovative, technology-driven solutions that elevate national tourism priorities into actionable community opportunities.

Q2: What were the main objectives of the project related to entrepreneurship and/or innovation?
The primary innovation objective was developing a centralized digital platform resolving seven critical pain points in Tunisian tourism: information fragmentation, lack of real-time verified data, absence of end-to-end trip planning, lack of pricing transparency, limited personalization, and inadequate visibility for emerging regional destinations. From an entrepreneurial standpoint, the project designed a sustainable business model based on performance commissions from partner bookings with hotels, travel agencies, and activity organizers. The initiative positioned JCI Sahline as an entrepreneurial innovation hub by engaging young leaders in product management, software engineering, spatial data analysis, and venture pitch defense.

Q3: Is the project plan clear and feasible?
Yes, our project followed a clear three-phase methodology. Phase 1 focused on market research, identifying market gaps across regional tourism assets, architecting geospatial datasets, and designing prototype user interfaces and financial projections. Phase 2 executed the technical demo and business model presentation at the SPARK & SHIP Tourism Hackathon on April 5, 2026, at Les Oliviers Family Club. Phase 3 established a dedicated follow-up committee to manage post-hackathon partner outreach, technical refinements, and transition toward incubation. Each phase defined specific team responsibilities, technological milestones, and budget controls.

Q4: Is the project implementation well structured, effective, and in line with the established plan?
Implementation was executed strictly according to plan. The technical committee delivered an operational prototype within deadline, showcasing interactive regional mapping and a responsive recommendation engine. At the SPARK & SHIP forum, our pitching team defended the venture before an expert jury comprising technology leaders and tourism professionals. The jury awarded Tunify Travel First Place among competing local chapters. Following the competition, JCI Sahline structured continuity by assigning dedicated officers to manage technical roadmap execution and initiate preliminary industry contacts.

Q5: To what extent does the project contribute to improving the financial situation of the local organization?
Winning First Place at the SPARK & SHIP Hackathon secured a direct cash prize of 600 TND sponsored by technology firm TRAE.ai, which was deposited into JCI Sahline's treasury. This capital injection strengthened our local operational fund without requiring member dues. Furthermore, the commercial model designed for Tunify Travel establishes a prospective recurring revenue stream through commission-sharing agreements with regional tourism operators, creating a sustainable funding mechanism for future community programs.

Team leaders honed agile project management, rapid prototyping under hackathon time constraints, and executive pitching skills before industry evaluators. Members strengthened technical abilities in full-stack web development and geospatial data analysis. Additionally, participating members gained direct experience in startup financial modeling, commission structuring, and professional business negotiations.

Community Impact

Q9: Are there concrete and measurable results (business creation, jobs, sustained innovation)?
The concrete results achieved during the reporting period include: developing a functional prototype platform; winning First Place at the SPARK & SHIP Hackathon on April 5, 2026; securing 600 TND in prize funding for the local organization; producing an online video demonstration reaching 115 views on YouTube; and presenting a viable digital business model to regional tourism stakeholders. Full-scale commercial deployment, job creation, and active merchant transactions represent the planned next stage following incubation.

Q10: What positive changes have been observed at the social or community level?
The project demonstrated to youth and local stakeholders in Sahline that young leaders can deliver advanced technological solutions to national economic challenges. It stimulated local interest in digital tourism, highlighted under-promoted cultural assets in the Monastir region, and created a collaborative working bridge between young technologists and established regional tourism agencies.

Q11: What have been the reactions or feedback from beneficiaries or partners?
The hackathon jury commended the platform's user interface, geospatial precision, and realistic business model by conferring the First Prize. Following public demonstrations, prominent regional travel agencies, specifically Wifak Tours and Easy Fly & Booking, praised the initiative and formally expressed interest in exploring strategic collaboration and supporting future platform deployment.

Q12: To what extent does the program promote social cohesion and community participation?
The project fostered exceptional inter-organization solidarity, bringing together members from nine participating JCI chapters (Sahline, Bennane, Ksibet Mediouni, Ouardanine, Bekalta, Khniss, Mahdia, Sayda, Msaken, and Ariana) around youth-led innovation. Within Sahline, it united university students, tech professionals, and local business actors around a collective vision for regional economic vitality.

Impact on the local organization

Q13: Has the project increased the visibility or reputation of your local organization?
Winning a competitive regional hackathon significantly elevated JCI Sahline's reputation across Zone C and the national organization. The victory was featured on digital channels, showcased via our YouTube demonstration video, and widely recognized among regional civic bodies and business sponsors as a benchmark of young entrepreneurial excellence.

Long-term impact of the program

Q15: Is there a planned continuation or long-term follow-up phase?

Q16: Have partnerships been consolidated to ensure the sustainability of the impact?
Initial technical partnership was established with sponsor TRAE.ai. Formalization of agreements with travel agencies Wifak Tours and Easy Fly & Booking is scheduled during the upcoming pilot phase through signed memorandums of understanding to validate the commercial commission model.

Q17: To what extent does the project contribute to the creation of local economic opportunities (jobs, businesses, services)?
By directing international and domestic travelers toward regional artisans, historic sites, local restaurants, and boutique accommodations in secondary destinations like Sahline and Maâtmeur, Tunify Travel creates decentralized economic opportunities, redistributing tourism expenditures beyond traditional enclave resorts to local micro-enterprises.
