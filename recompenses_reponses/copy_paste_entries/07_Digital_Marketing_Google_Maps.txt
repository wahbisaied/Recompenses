Objectives, Planning, and Execution of the Marketing Plan

Q1: What were the main objectives of your marketing strategy for the year?
JCI Sahline's 2026 marketing strategy pursued four core objectives: First, modernizing chapter branding through high-definition visual storytelling and mobile-optimized video formats to showcase community actions. Second, expanding digital reach beyond our immediate member network, specifically targeting non-followers across Monastir Governorate. Third, pioneering a localized search engine optimization (SEO) initiative via Google Maps to index essential civic, educational, and commercial infrastructure for residents and visitors. Fourth, establishing a multi-channel dissemination architecture spanning social media (Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube), broadcast media (Radio Monastir), and our official web portal to drive membership growth and elevate sponsorship appeal.

Q2: How was your communication plan structured (targets, channels, messages, timeline)?
The plan was structured into four distinct audience tiers: 1) Local youth and university students targeted via Instagram Reels and Facebook with messages of active citizenship and leadership development; 2) Business sponsors, professionals, and alumni targeted via LinkedIn with achievements in governance and enterprise projects; 3) General citizens, families, and residents reached via Facebook and local radio with public health and cultural service announcements; and 4) Regional tourists and newcomers addressed through Google Maps local directory listings. The timeline followed a monthly editorial rhythm aligned with major chapter milestones—ranging from the February Finance Law seminar to May Heritage Month and autumn public health campaigns.

Q3: What types of content or campaigns were implemented?
We executed four integrated campaign tracks: 1) Major Project Campaigns, featuring dedicated promotional teasers, video reels, and live broadcasts for flagship events (October Rose, Month of Heritage, Rabies Vaccination); 2) Local Community SEO (Discover Sahline), systematically adding and reviewing over 535 local schools, public services, and businesses on Google Maps; 3) Institutional and Leadership Spotlights, highlighting member graduations, national assembly delegations, and executive training workshops; and 4) Civic Education and Cultural Preservation, utilizing interactive stories, educational carousels, and radio interviews to disseminate vital public information.

Q4: What planning tools did you use (e.g., editorial calendar, retro-planning)?
Our digital communications team utilized an integrated suite of management tools: an annual Notion editorial calendar mapping campaigns against international UN observances and JCI priorities; Meta Business Suite for scheduling cross-platform publications on Facebook and Instagram; Google Drive repositories for raw media archiving and visual asset staging; and Canva Pro alongside generative AI tools for agile graphic production and copywriting optimization.

Q5: Was the plan followed as intended? What adjustments were necessary during the year?
The plan was executed with high discipline, achieving all major milestone campaigns. During the year, analytics indicated that short-form video reels and photographic albums generated 290% higher reach than static link posts. In response, we dynamically shifted our resource allocation in the third quarter toward high-retention video production, professional photography (partnering with Aïcha Studio), and real-time live reporting, which directly propelled our Facebook page past 4,000 followers and catalyzed viral visibility on health campaigns.

Q9: How has the digital strategy helped to reach new audiences or potential members? If yes, explain how.
The digital strategy was engineered specifically to pierce the algorithmic bubble of existing members. On Facebook, 76.5% of our 400,000 views came from non-followers, exposing tens of thousands of regional citizens to JCI's active citizenship mission for the first time. The viral dissemination of our October Rose campaign (where 93.3% of the 61,000 viewers were non-followers) and our active Instagram bio link tree (connecting directly to member registration forms) directly expanded our recruitment pipeline, converting online observers into active chapter applicants.

The team actively engaged commenters, reposted partner stories, tagged participating institutions and sponsors, and moderated public discussions to maintain an inspiring, professional brand environment.

Originality and relevance of content created (text, visuals, videos) tailored to the chosen platforms

Q19: What types of content did you produce (videos, interviews, visuals, stories, articles, reels, etc.)?
Our production portfolio encompassed diverse multimedia formats: high-impact vertical video Reels; professional photo carousels; live event video streams; graphic informational posters and flyers; formal radio studio interviews; long-form webinar recordings on YouTube; and written editorial tributes celebrating member milestones.

Q20: How was this content adapted to different platforms?
Content was rigorously customized per platform: Facebook was leveraged for narrative storytelling, photo albums, and community event notices; Instagram prioritized sleek, aesthetic vertical reels and interactive story polls; LinkedIn focused on professional development, corporate partnerships, and governance milestones; YouTube archived comprehensive project demonstrations and training sessions; and Google Maps delivered concise, factual geographic data and location reviews.

Creativity was reflected in our ability to make serious civic topics engaging and shareable.
