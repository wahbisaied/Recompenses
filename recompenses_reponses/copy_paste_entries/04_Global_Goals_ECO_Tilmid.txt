Project Purpose: Describe the main objective or goal of the project. (Word limit:100):
ECO Tilmid establishes an innovative circular economy model within the educational sector of Sahline. Initiated in May 2026, the project mobilizes students, teachers, and school administrations to collect used textbooks, notebooks, and paper archives for recycling. The proceeds generated from selling collected paper to a licensed industrial recycler directly finance the purchase of brand-new notebooks for school children from low-income families. The project advances United Nations Sustainable Development Goals 4 (Quality Education) and 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production) by converting environmental waste management into practical, dignity-affirming social solidarity.

Project Overview: Provide a brief description of your project, outlining its main objectives, key activities, and expected impacts. (Word limit:150):
Launched in May 2026 across public and private educational institutions in Sahline, ECO Tilmid addresses end-of-year paper waste through social entrepreneurship. JCI Sahline partnered with five educational establishments (École and Collège AMAD, Lycée and Collège Lambda, École Primaire Salem, Lycée Sahline, and Collège Sahline) and an industrial partner, Société Kahlaoui de Recyclage. JCI members conducted environmental awareness visits, installed dedicated paper collection boxes in schools, and coordinated weighing and transport logistics. With an intermediate milestone of 150 kg collected toward a one-ton objective, the project generates financial resources to buy new school supplies for vulnerable students, transforming environmental stewardship into an engine of educational equity.

Program Objectives and Strategic Execution

Q1: What were the primary objectives of the program? (Word limit:200):
The program pursued three strategic objectives. First, advancing SDG 12 by diverting educational paper waste from municipal dumps into formalized recycling channels, cultivating environmental responsibility and waste segregation habits among school pupils. Second, supporting SDG 4 by creating a self-sustaining funding mechanism: proceeds from collected paper (contracted at 0.400 TND per kilogram) directly fund the acquisition of new school notebooks for students from impoverished backgrounds, alleviating back-to-school financial pressure on vulnerable families. Third, establishing a collaborative educational ecosystem uniting private schools, public institutions, recycling enterprises, and youth leaders in a joint circular economy initiative.

Q2: Describe the key strategies and methods used in the execution of the program. (Word limit:200):
The project deployed a structured four-stage methodology. Stage 1 focused on institutional outreach: securing administrative authorizations from five public and private schools and establishing a collection agreement with Société Kahlaoui de Recyclage. Stage 2 executed on-campus engagement: JCI members visited classrooms to deliver age-appropriate sensitization talks on circular economy and installed informative campaign posters funded by partner École AMAD. Stage 3 established collection infrastructure: positioning marked sorting boxes in each school to gather used notebooks, scrap paper, and outdated archives. Stage 4 managed logistics: organizing periodic collection, batch weighing, and commercial transfer to the recycling company, with all revenues escrowed for notebook purchases.

Q3: In what ways did the program cultivate enterprising young leaders for a changing world? (Word limit:200):
ECO Tilmid empowered JCI Sahline members to act as green social entrepreneurs. Rather than conducting traditional charity drives, members designed an operational circular business model requiring commercial negotiations, supply-chain logistics, and multi-stakeholder coordination. Members negotiated material pricing with industrial recyclers, designed educational campaigns tailored to youth audiences, and managed distributed collection points across five distinct school campuses. This hands-on experience honed competencies in sustainable development planning, operational project management, and cross-sector leadership.

Q4: How did this initiative align with the JCI Plan of Action and the strategic direction towards enterprising leadership? (Word limit:200):
The initiative directly aligns with the JCI Plan of Action by addressing global United Nations priorities at a hyper-local level through enterprising solutions. By rejecting passive aid models in favor of resource recovery, JCI Sahline demonstrated how young leaders apply business principles—valuing recyclable waste, establishing commercial partnerships, and reinvesting proceeds—to resolve acute community challenges, perfectly embodying JCI's vision of enterprising leadership driving sustainable societal change.

Planning, Execution, and Financial Oversight

Q5: Describe the execution process. How were the project's activities coordinated to align with the financial goals? (Word limit:200):
Execution strictly maintained a zero-cost operational structure. JCI Sahline absorbed minor organizational coordination costs, while our educational partner, École et Collège AMAD, covered all graphic design, printing, and promotional poster expenses. The financial model was tied directly to recovery volume: each kilogram of paper recovered generates 0.400 TND in revenue. This transparent unit-pricing structure ensured that project revenues directly mapped to educational purchasing power, creating a fully traceable social return on investment.

Q6: How effectively were the budget and financial resources managed to achieve the desired outcomes? (Word limit:200):
Financial oversight was highly rigorous. The project maintained strict segregation between organizational expenses and recycling revenues. All proceeds from the sale of collected paper were quarantined in a dedicated social solidarity ledger earmarked solely for notebook procurement. By securing commercial transport and corporate sponsorship for communication materials, 100% of the revenue generated from paper recycling directly translates into educational materials for vulnerable children.

Q7: What was the financial outcome of the project, whether surplus or deficit? How will any financial surplus be reinvested to develop your local organization? (Word limit:200):
The project operates as a dedicated social fund with zero deficit. Current collections have achieved 150 kg of recovered paper, generating an initial 60 TND toward our ultimate 400 TND target (corresponding to the 1,000 kg milestone). All financial surplus generated beyond the purchase of notebooks is reinvested into expanding the recycling bin infrastructure for the upcoming academic year.

Ten JCI Sahline members actively executed the project. Responsibilities were distributed across key functions: school administration liaison and institutional permissions; design and presentation of classroom awareness modules; distribution and maintenance of sorting boxes; logistical supervision of paper transport and official weighing with Société Kahlaoui; and accounting verification of recycling proceeds and procurement.

Team members strengthened their abilities in public speaking before young audiences, gained practical knowledge in waste valorization economics, and established strong professional relationships with school administrators and environmental enterprise managers.
