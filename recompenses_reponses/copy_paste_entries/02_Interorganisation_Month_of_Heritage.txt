Month of Heritage & Medina Renovation - JCI Sahline

Describe the main objective or goal of the project. (Word limit:150):
The primary goal of the Month of Heritage celebration was uniting public authorities, cultural institutions, civic associations, and local artists into an integrated coalition to revitalize and celebrate the historical, architectural, and artisanal heritage of Sahline and Maâtmer. Running from May 8 to 10, 2026, the program aimed to transform neglected urban spaces into lively cultural hubs, transmit intangible traditions and craftsmanship to younger generations, and initiate the tangible urban revitalization of the ancient medina center at Zekak Laaribeiin. By aligning ten distinct institutional and civic stakeholders, JCI Sahline sought to demonstrate how collective civil-society leadership can safeguard regional identity while laying the groundwork for sustainable local cultural tourism.

Provide a brief description of your project, outlining its main objectives, key activities, and expected impacts. (Word limit:100):
From May 8 to 10, 2026, JCI Sahline coordinated a three-day regional heritage festival across Sahline and Maâtmer in partnership with over ten civic and governmental entities. Key activities included an opening street folklore carnival, traditional artisan and costume exhibitions, hands-on craft workshops, intellectual symposiums on architectural preservation, archaeological exhibits, and the physical rehabilitation of the ancient Zekak Laaribeiin alleyway in central Sahline with blue-and-white murals and a traditional cultural café. The project engaged hundreds of local citizens, deepened civic pride, and reinforced inter-organizational cooperation for heritage preservation.

Program Goals and Leadership Development Alignment

Q1: What were the primary goals of this program?(Word limit:200):
The program pursued four interconnected strategic goals. First, establishing a formal inter-organizational coalition between municipal authorities, cultural delegations, civic clubs, and youth organizations to jointly safeguard regional heritage. Second, raising community awareness regarding local architectural and historical wealth through academic symposiums, rare documentary exhibits, and curated heritage discovery tours in Sahline, Maâtmer, and Sidi Ameur. Third, engaging children and young citizens through interactive workshops in traditional pottery, calligraphy, painting, and folklore to ensure the generational transmission of intangible craftsmanship. Fourth, executing a concrete urban renewal initiative by restoring and embellishing the historic medina alleyway of Zekak Laaribeiin, enhancing aesthetic standards and creating community-led tourism appeal.

Q2: In what ways did the program cultivate enterprising young leaders for a changing world?(Word limit:200):
The festival served as an intensive leadership laboratory for JCI Sahline members. Organizing a multi-venue, three-day municipal festival required young leaders to navigate complex inter-agency negotiations, align differing organizational cultures, and secure logistical alignment across regional delegations, municipal councils, and security services. Members took full ownership of event logistics, media relations, protocol coordination with government officials, artist curation, and street crowd management. By successfully arbitrating cross-sector partnerships between public bureaucrats and grassroots artisans, our members cultivated enterprise diplomacy, crisis management, and stakeholder engagement skills essential for impactful community leadership.

Q3: How did this initiative align with the JCI Plan of Action and the strategic direction towards enterprising leadership?(Word limit:200):
The project directly reflected the JCI Plan of Action by positioning JCI Sahline not merely as an event organizer, but as a strategic civic convener uniting government, civil society, and the private sphere. Enterprising leadership was demonstrated by transforming traditional cultural preservation into a multi-sector economic and urban development platform. Planning, Execution, and Financial Oversight

Q4: Describe the execution process. How were the project's activities coordinated to align with the financial goals?(Word limit:200):
Execution followed a rigorous multi-stakeholder timeline coordinated through weekly inter-organizational liaison meetings. JCI Sahline mapped every operational requirement against partner capabilities to optimize resource allocation without cash deficits. Day 1 featured the street folklore carnival from Talaat Sidi Nasr and the opening of traditional craft exhibitions at the Maison de la Culture. Day 2 deployed youth artistic workshops under the theme "Houmtna Fen" and historical colloquiums at Maâtmer. Day 3 convened architectural preservation symposiums and guided field tours. Logistical and financial responsibilities were strategically shared: municipal services provided infrastructure and permits; cultural centers provided venues and technical equipment; and local businesses and associations contributed materials.

Financial management was centered on resource optimization and in-kind partnership mobilization. By securing venue spaces, audio equipment, municipal staging, and artist honorariums through institutional partnerships with the Maison de la Culture, Public Library, and Sahline Municipality, direct out-of-pocket expenses were minimized. Material costs for the physical renovation of Zekak Laaribeiin (paint, brushes, restoration supplies) were supported through local community contributions and civic sponsorships. All expenses and donations were tracked in event ledgers, ensuring complete fiscal transparency and zero deficit across the three days.

Q6: What was the financial outcome of the project, whether surplus or deficit? How will any financial surplus be reinvested to develop your local organization? (Word limit:200):
The project achieved a balanced financial outcome with zero deficit, as all major logistical, venue, and technical expenses were directly absorbed or provided in-kind by partner institutions. The economic value generated through mobilized resources, venue waivers, and municipal equipment significantly exceeded standalone chapter funding capacities. Any indirect savings achieved in chapter operational accounts were allocated to JCI Sahline's leadership training fund and future community development actions.
