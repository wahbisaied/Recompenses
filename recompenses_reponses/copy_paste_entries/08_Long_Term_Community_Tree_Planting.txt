Project objectives, planning, and execution

Q1: To what extent is the project relevant to the objectives and priorities of the target category?
Green Sahline–Maâtmeur addresses the environmental and public health need for green spaces across the industrial and transit areas of the Sahline delegation. Launched on National Tree Day (November 8, 2026), the initiative establishes a structured afforestation framework designed to green primary schools, civic courtyards, and degraded peripheral zones adjacent to regional industrial parks, salt flats, and the Monastir technopole. The project aligns with long-term community development by instituting a participatory model where municipal authorities, state agricultural delegates, local scouts, and private nurseries co-invest in sustainable urban afforestation and ongoing botanical maintenance.

Q2: What planning strategy did you follow (steps, partners, resources)?
Our strategy followed a four-step framework: 1) Site assessment and institutional coordination with the Municipal Council of Sahline–Maâtmeur and primary school administrations; 2) Resource mobilization uniting regional public bodies, including the National Waste Management Agency (ANGED), the Regional Commissariat for Agricultural Development (CRDA Monastir), and the Sahline Agricultural Extension Cell; 3) Private sector sponsorship with Pépinière Idéale contributing 20 ornamental plants; and 4) Community engagement involving the Scouts of Maâtmeur, the Women's Union, youth houses, and local district leaders (omdas) for planting and long-term caretaking.

Q3: What was the overall budget for the project, and what sources of funding were mobilized?
The initiative operated on a shared-resource partnership model. Pépinière Idéale provided an in-kind donation of 20 mixed ornamental plants. The Sahline–Maâtmeur Municipality contributed baseline logistical and municipal equipment support, while JCI Sahline provided organizational coordination and hosted the digital campaign portal. The campaign solicits ongoing citizen tree contributions to fund future sites without requiring commercial debt.

Q4: To what extent does the project contain objectively verifiable indicators for evaluating results and include a clear evaluation plan?
Verifiable indicators established at launch include: 130 total plants installed on site, comprising 100 myoporum shrubs, 10 ficus trees, and 20 mixed ornamental plants; one primary school yard (École 2 Mars in Maâtmeur) fully re-vegetated; and an active digital campaign page on our website (https://jci-sahline.tn/jci-sahline-plantation). Our ongoing evaluation protocol mandates quarterly botanical audit visits to track irrigation consistency and plant survival rates at 3, 6, and 12 months.

Member participation.

Q5: How many JCI members were actively involved in the project?
Eight JCI Sahline members actively coordinated and executed the launching phase of the afforestation campaign.

Q6: What roles did members play throughout the program?
Members managed site coordination with the director of École 2 Mars Maâtmeur, secured institutional permissions with the regional agricultural delegation, organized transport of plants from Pépinière Idéale, led citizen planting teams alongside the Scouts, and created the digital campaign portal on the chapter's official website.

Q7: To what extent does the program encourage member involvement in the planning and implementation of community actions?
The program actively involved members in hands-on ecological planning, land preparation, and civic mobilization. Working alongside agricultural technicians and municipal workers provided members with direct experience in ecological project management and civic diplomacy.

Community impact

Q8: To what extent does the program meet the priority needs of the local community?
The Sahline-Maâtmeur region is characterized by intensive industrial, textile, and transit traffic, leading to significant soil degradation and a documented deficit of shaded green spaces. Greening public schoolyards directly improves children's daily educational environment while fostering environmental awareness and shared civic responsibility among residents.

Q9: What measurable changes has the project brought to the target community?
The tangible physical result achieved at launch was the planting of 130 botanical units across the grounds of École Primaire 2 Mars in Maâtmeur (100 myoporums, 10 ficus, and 20 ornamental plants), creating an immediate perimeter windbreak and green educational landscape where barren soil previously existed.

Q10: How many direct and indirect beneficiaries have been reached?
Direct beneficiaries include the 350 primary school pupils, teachers, and administrative staff at École 2 Mars who immediately benefit from improved school grounds. Indirect beneficiaries encompass the broader neighborhood residents and community volunteers participating in the greening campaign.

Q11: Have you received feedback from the community or external partners?
School administrators, parents, and participating Scouts expressed sincere appreciation for transforming the schoolyard into a greener space. Regional agricultural delegates commended the initiative for promoting drought-resilient Mediterranean plant varieties (myoporum and ficus) well-suited to the local coastal climate.

Q12: Does the project meet the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)? If so, which ones?"
Yes, the project directly advances SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities and Communities) through urban greening, SDG 13 (Climate Action) by enhancing local micro-climates, and SDG 15 (Life on Land) through vegetation restoration and soil stabilization.

Impact on the local organization

Q13: To what extent is the program likely to have a lasting and significant impact on beneficiaries, the local organization, and the community?
The lasting impact is secured through school-based caretaking agreements ensuring regular irrigation by staff and pupils. For JCI Sahline, the project establishes a multi-year ecological action platform that positions the chapter as a credible partner for regional environmental agencies and international climate grants.

Q14: To what extent does the project contribute to strengthening the local organization's brand awareness? (Visibility, engagement of the local community and stakeholders, brand image).
The campaign demonstrated JCI Sahline's capability to convene high-level regional stakeholders—including the Delegation, the Local Council, the Regional Agriculture Commissariat, and the Ministry of Education—enhancing our reputation as a unifying civic catalyst.

Q15: Did the event receive effective media coverage on various social networks?
The campaign was promoted through a dedicated portal on JCI Sahline's website (https://jci-sahline.tn/jci-sahline-plantation) and broadcast through event invitations and photo announcements across our Facebook and Instagram channels, reaching thousands of local citizens.

Q16: What long-term effects are anticipated from this project?
Anticipated long-term effects include mature canopy shade and wind protection at partner schools, decreased localized dust pollution, and the expansion of the greening network to additional public spaces and cultural institutions across the Sahline delegation.
